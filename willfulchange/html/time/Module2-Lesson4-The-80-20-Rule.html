What this lens helps you see

The 80/20 Rule, also called the Pareto Principle, suggests that a small number of activities may create a large share of the results.

  • The vital few are the activities most strongly connected to the result you want.
  • Finding them helps you direct more attention toward high-impact work.
  • The split is a guide, not a claim that every situation follows an exact mathematical ratio.

How to apply the 80/20 Rule

Move through the four steps to connect your task list to a specific result.

Step 1: List tasks and responsibilities

Write down the activities connected to the outcome, including routine work and work that may have a larger effect.

Example: customer growth and revenue

Goal: Increase my customer base by 50% and projected revenue by 20% by the end of Q4 2025.

Task list:

  1. Managing and responding to emails
  2. Calendar management
  3. Marketing and prospecting
  4. Data entry
  5. Maintenance of company website
  6. Creating trainings
  7. Accounting
  8. Business goal setting
  9. Obtaining client testimonials and feedback
  10. Forecasting and budgeting

Step 2: Define the goal or desired outcome

Be specific about the result you want. Use the goal from Step 1 as the standard for deciding which activities are likely to matter most.

Step 3: Identify the vital few

Compare the task list with the goal. Identify the small group of activities most strongly aligned with the goal and most likely to produce meaningful impact.

For this example: Marketing and prospecting and creating trainings are the vital few because they have the clearest connection to attracting customers and increasing revenue. Business goal setting, testimonials, and forecasting may also support the goal.

Step 4: Decide what to do with the rest

Protect time for the vital few. For the remaining work, decide whether to schedule it, delegate it, automate it, reduce it, or keep it because the context requires it.

Attention shift: Block time for marketing, prospecting, and training. Allocate email blocks; automate calendar management with a CRM; delegate data entry to an automated system and website maintenance to a virtual assistant; schedule accounting monthly, goal setting quarterly, and forecasting for a specific date.

Use cadence: Review the 80/20 lens monthly or annually, and sooner when your goal, circumstances, or available resources change.
