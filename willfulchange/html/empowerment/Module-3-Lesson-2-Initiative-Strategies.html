Lesson 2: Strategies for Fostering a Culture of Initiative

Five strategies that foster initiative

Move through the carousel to review one strategy at a time. Notice which approaches create clarity, reduce barriers, build confidence, or give people more room to act.

1: Build Trust

Create a safer foundation for initiative.

Try this: Listen to your staff. Communicate clearly and often. Admit your mistakes, show appreciation, be consistent, be vulnerable, and be patient.

2: Implement Feedback

Use employees’ needs to shape useful support.

Try this: Ask employees what they need and how you might help them. This can empower them to complete tasks more quickly.

3: Offer Professional Development Opportunities

Build skills and confidence for meaningful goals.

Try this: Use individual plans, offer professional training, remove barriers, and provide effective coaching.

4: Help to Educate

Make knowledge easy to access and use.

Try this: Make instructional materials and resources easily accessible to employees to increase autonomy. Keep shared resources current so employees can find the information they need.

5: Demonstrate Appreciation & Recognize Limitations

Reinforce contribution without creating overload.

Try this: Express appreciation to help people continue working efficiently and improve morale. Recognize employee limitations so they are not overwhelmed and can receive more meaningful tasks that support their professional goals.

Make support usable

Match the strategy to the person and situation. Current resources, meaningful tasks, and awareness of capacity can help people contribute with greater autonomy.
