Identifying Leadership Potential in Team Members:

Review three facts about implicit models and bias when identifying high-potential employees.

Fact 1:

When focusing on leadership potential, most managers have an implicit model in their head as to what a high-potential employee looks like.

Fact 2:

Implicit models can be fraught with biases.

Fact 3:

Implicit models can be based on personal biases and/or incorrect beliefs and assumptions about what it takes to be a future leader.

What Should We Look For to Identify Leadership Potential?

Use the carousel to review indicators covered in this module and the settings where they may be observed.

Effective Interpersonal Skills

Effectively manage and work with others.

Can be clearly seen across a variety of exchanges and team meetings.

The ability to “Connect the Dots”

Think broad and strategically about the business, as well as the external world around themselves.

Can be spotted in team meetings/brainstorming sessions, or when your staff is giving a presentation.

Ability to learn from experiences and apply those learnings to new situations

This can be observed in action during daily work, presentations, operations and meetings.

Ambition, motivation and drive to deliver great results and have an impact at more senior levels.

This is demonstrated by staff taking on work above and beyond their current role and/or of those more senior to them.
