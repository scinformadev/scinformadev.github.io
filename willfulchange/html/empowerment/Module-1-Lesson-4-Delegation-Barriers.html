Mistakes to Avoid

Compare each delegation mistake with a more effective approach. Click a mistake to see the details.

1. Failing to express confidence and empower employees

What the mistake looks like

“Before you train the staff on Excel, send me your curriculum and check with me before making any decisions.” The leader becomes a bottleneck.

A better approach

“I trust you to plan and lead the Excel training. Make the decisions you need to, and let me know what support would help.”

2. Failing to plan how to monitor progress

What the mistake looks like

“Go ahead with the training. I’ll check in periodically.” The employee has no clear plan for when progress will be reviewed.

A better approach

“I’ll attend one training session each week and share feedback afterward. We’ll use that time to discuss progress and any support you need.”

3. Delegating only routine administrative tasks

What the mistake looks like

A capable employee who wants to contribute more is repeatedly assigned only routine data entry. Their skills and interest in growing go unused.

A better approach

Offer a suitable challenge, such as helping design the Excel training, with the resources and authority needed to succeed.

Cost of Not Delegating

When leaders take on more work, they can become overwhelmed and stressed. They may burn out completely and leave.

DDI's Global Leadership Forecast 2023 found that 70% of leaders under age 35 report feeling used up at the end of every day, with rates even higher among women and minority leaders.

“Never confuse the cost of something with its value.” — Robin Hobbs

Chart source: Development Dimensions International, Inc. 2023.
