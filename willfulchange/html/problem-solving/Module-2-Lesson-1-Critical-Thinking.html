Critical thinking

An analytical approach to problem solving and decision-making. Leaders who learn critical thinking can rise above biases and assumptions that often hinder decision-making by recognizing assumptions, evaluating arguments, and drawing informed conclusions, which enhance their decision-making ability.

Importance

It involves assessing current skills, fostering self-awareness and emotional intelligence, and guiding leaders toward improvement. Ultimately, critical thinking allows leaders to eliminate distractions and make well-informed decisions, benefiting both the organization and its employees.

Five-step critical thinking process

Step 1: Identify the problem

Before diving into critical thinking, leaders must clearly define the problem they’re solving. Consider different perspectives and ask questions like, “What’s happening?”

Example

A project manager faces declining customer satisfaction. They identify the problem as inefficient customer support processes.

Step 2: Research

Gather relevant information from various sources. Explore data, research papers, case studies, and expert opinions related to the problem.

Example

A sales director researches industry trends, competitor strategies, and customer preferences to improve sales performance.

Step 3: Determine data relevance

Filter out irrelevant or biased information. Focus on data that directly impacts the problem.

Example

An HR manager evaluates employee engagement survey results, emphasizing areas directly linked to retention and productivity.

Step 4: Ask questions

Curiosity drives critical thinking. Leaders should ask probing questions, challenge assumptions, and seek deeper insights.

Example

A team leader asks, “Why are our project timelines consistently delayed?” to uncover underlying issues.

Step 5: Identify the best solution

Consider all available information and generate potential solutions. Evaluate each option based on data, feasibility, and impact.

Example

A product manager weighs the pros and cons of launching a new feature versus improving existing functionality.
